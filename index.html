Why an alliance

The talent isn't missing. The connections are.

Greater Philadelphia has no shortage of creative organisations. What it lacks is anything joining them up. Each one carries its own website, its own mailing list, its own promotion — usually on one overworked person — and a funder or client trying to understand the scene has no single place to look. Meanwhile the organisation three neighbourhoods over is solving the exact same problem from scratch.

There is already a body that represents the sector, convenes it, and moves grant money through it. That isn't this. We don't lobby. We build. The Alliance is the working layer: a shared front door, referrals between members, and infrastructure that member organisations can actually pick up and use. No cut of anyone's work, no gatekeeping, and free for artists permanently.

Keep it free

Back the wall.

Artist membership is free and stays free. Keeping it that way costs something — hosting, printing badges and stickers, and the time that goes into the wall.

Money is the obvious way to help. It isn't the only one, and right now it isn't even the most useful one.

Ways to help

  • Space A room for a meet-up, a wall, a window.
  • Print Badges, stickers, and signage at cost.
  • Referrals Introduce an organisation that should be on the wall.
  • Money Covers hosting and materials.
Chip in

Donations aren't switched on yet — the Alliance needs an entity to receive them first. Until then, the four routes above are live and the fastest is a referral.

The shared mark

Wear the tile on your own site.

Every member — organisation or artist — drops this badge in their footer, linking back to the wall. That reciprocal link is what turns scattered sites into one network, and it quietly lifts everyone's local search.
